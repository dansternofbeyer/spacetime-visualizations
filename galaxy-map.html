Physics notes

Redshift. The catalog lists cz, defined from the measured redshift z with the catalog's own value c = 299 792.5 km/s:

ΛCDM comoving distance (flat universe, Planck 2018: H₀ = 67.66, Ωm = 0.30966 + Ων, ΩΛ = 0.68885, Ωγ = 5.4×10⁻⁵):

This is how far the galaxy is today. Hogg (1999), arXiv:astro-ph/9905116, eq. 15. Massive neutrinos are counted as matter, which is accurate to about 10⁻⁵ at these redshifts.

Hubble law: . It's the low-redshift limit of the formula above. At z = 0.05 it overestimates the distance by about 1.2%. Pick a galaxy to see the difference.

What this map is not.

• Redshift space, not true space. A galaxy's own motion (hundreds of km/s, up to ~1000 km/s in clusters) adds to cz. Clusters therefore look stretched toward us ("fingers of God"), and distances below ~2000 km/s are unreliable.

• Brightness limited. The survey only includes galaxies brighter than about magnitude 15.5, so far-away regions look emptier than they are.

• Empty wedges are areas the survey didn't cover, or that dust in the Milky Way hides (the "Zone of Avoidance"), not real voids.

• Velocities are heliocentric. Correcting to the CMB rest frame would shift them by up to ~370 km/s.

• Galaxies with negative cz (blueshifted, in the Local Group) can't be placed this way and are left out.
